Research Statement

AI and machine learning for cancer biology and One Health diagnostics.

My research focuses on using machine learning to identify diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic candidates for aggressive companion animal cancers, with significant translational potential for human cancers. I also lead innovative AI-assisted OMICs approaches for pathogen identification and molecular characterization in One Health.

Current focus

My current work centers on the tumor microenvironment in aggressive animal cancers, including osteosarcoma and mammary carcinoma, with the long-term goal of developing innovative immunotherapies that improve outcomes in companion animals and inform human oncology.

Personal Statement

Research trajectory

This page reflects the full arc of my work: microbial pathogenesis, diagnostic laboratory leadership, metagenomics, AI-driven bioinformatics, and translational cancer research.

Foundations

Host-pathogen interactions and vaccine development

My research journey began with host-pathogen interactions, including a pivotal role in developing a patented vaccine composition targeting multidrug-resistant Staphylococcus pseudintermedius. This work advanced our understanding of resistance mechanisms and bacterial pathogenesis while sharpening my skills in molecular cloning, CRISPR-based microbial genome editing, flow cytometry, and in vivo studies using mouse and canine models.

Diagnostics

Director, Diagnostic Immunology & Virology Laboratories

I directed both the Diagnostic Immunology Laboratory and the Virology Diagnostic Laboratory, overseeing immunophenotyping for blood cancers, molecular monitoring during chemotherapy, and serological and molecular testing for viral diseases in domestic and wild animals.

Current Program

Comparative oncology, AI, and immunotherapy

My cross-disciplinary work now explores the tumor microenvironment in aggressive animal cancers using genomics, proteomics, metabolomics, CRISPR screening, predictive modeling, and AI-assisted bioinformatics. Inspired by the parallels between microbes, the microbiome, and tumor biology, I also investigate microbial proteins as direct cytotoxic agents and modulators of the immune response while leading a state-funded drug repurposing project aimed at developing new cancer therapies.

Contributions To Science

Three recurring scientific contributions

My research contributions consistently bridge sequencing, OMICs, microbial pathogenesis, diagnostics, biomarker discovery, and immunotherapy-oriented translation.

Contribution 01

Next-generation sequencing and genomic resource building

I use Illumina and Oxford Nanopore platforms to study eukaryotic and prokaryotic genomes, contribute to genomic databases, and combine sequencing with bioinformatics to resolve virulence factors, antibiotic resistance, and evolutionary relationships. This work also includes training students and faculty and publishing data that expands shared genomics knowledge.

Contribution 02

OMICs-driven biomarkers and immunotherapy insight

By integrating genomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics, I investigate biomarkers and immunotherapy opportunities in viral infections and cancer. This work aims to clarify immune response biology and support targeted, personalized therapeutic strategies.

Contribution 03

Virulence factor characterization and translational vaccine work

Through in vitro and in vivo studies using flow cytometry, ELISA, CRISPR, and related molecular and serological techniques, I have characterized virulence factors in multiple microbial agents. This includes identifying key antigens for a patented vaccine composition against multidrug-resistant Staphylococcus pseudintermedius.
